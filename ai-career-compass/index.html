Belief 1

"90% of Anthropic's code is written by AI."

Partly true

Right: Anthropic's own conservative figure is more than 80% of merged lines as of May 2026; leadership's 90%+ only counts when scripts and experimental code are included.

Wrong: in Oct 2025 Redwood Research estimated about 50% of merged lines, and Dario Amodei said "not uniformly". Every line is reviewed by engineers, and Anthropic's engineering headcount doubled in 2025.

Belief 2

"75% of Google's code is AI."

True, for new code

Right: Sundar Pichai, 22 Apr 2026: 75% of all new code at Google is "AI-generated and approved by engineers", up from 25% (Oct 2024), 30% (Apr 2025) and about 50% (fall 2025).

Caveat: it is new code, not the whole codebase, it includes autocomplete-style acceptances, and Google has never published how it measures it.

Belief 3

"Within months it'll be 100% AI-written, so programmers are done."

Partly true, misleading

Right: writing code is near-automated at frontier labs. The damage is real and lands on entry-level jobs: Stanford's "Canaries" paper finds −13% to −20% employment for ages 22–25 in exposed work; Indeed software postings are 27.5% below Feb 2020, with 71% of the rebound in senior roles; TCS guides to about 25,000 freshers against its usual 40,000.

Wrong: review, judgment and research taste are now the bottleneck. In METR's 2025 trial, experienced developers were 19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster.

Belief 4

"There's not much competition in AI safety."

Partly true

Right: only about 1,100 FTE (≈600 technical) worked on it worldwide in 2025 (McAleese); under 10,000 in total (80,000 Hours); roughly 1 technical safety person per 2,000–5,000 AI workers. Funding is rising fast: Coefficient Giving $168M (2024) → $351M (2025) → over $1B (2026).

Wrong: entry slots are very selective. ARENA picks ~30 of ~700 applicants; Anthropic Fellows accepts ~1.6%. Low headcount means the field is short of people who can show work, not that entry is easy.

Belief 5

"My aerospace friend was right."

Mostly false, in India

Right: AI exposure is lower than in software, and the space economy is growing: $8.4B toward a $44B target by 2033, ~440 space startups, Skyroot valued at $1.1B.

Wrong: jobs are scarce. ISRO gets ~1.5 lakh applications per 100 vacancies and advertised 175–176 engineer posts in 2026; GATE Aerospace had 8,444 registrations against 2,59,922 for CS; IIST cut BTech aerospace seats from 75 to 63; starting pay is lower. The best-placed engineers combine AI and aerospace.

Belief 6

"Education isn't worth it: AI is smarter than teachers and information is free."

Partly true

Right: an AI tutor designed to make students think more than doubled learning gains (Harvard physics RCT, Sci Rep 2025).

Wrong: careless use hurts: students scored 17% worse once ChatGPT was removed (Bastani, PNAS 2025); MIT's "cognitive debt" preprint and the OECD's "metacognitive laziness" warning agree. o3 hallucinated 33% and o4-mini 48% on PersonQA. Degrees still gate visas (H-1B, German Blue Card), and skills-based hiring changed under 1 in 700 hires. Information is free; structure, feedback, credentials and people are not.

Hype vs reality: AI writing code

Writing code is being automated. Deciding what to build, verifying it and keeping AI safe is where humans move.

The headline percentages are real but count AI-drafted, human-reviewed lines. Companies won't say how they measure them.

  1. Mar 2025

    Anthropic: Amodei predicts AI will write 90% of code "in three to six months", and "essentially all" in twelve. A forecast, not a measurement.

  2. Apr 2025

    • Microsoft: Nadella says "maybe 20%, 30%" of code in its repos is written by software. No newer official figure through Oct 2026.
    • Meta: Zuckerberg forecasts "maybe half" of development by AI within a year.

  3. Oct 2025

    Anthropic: at Dreamforce Amodei says 90% holds "on many teams… not uniformly". Redwood Research estimates ~50% of merged lines company-wide.

  4. Mar 2026

    Meta: internal targets: 65% of engineers in one org writing over 75% of committed code with AI in H1 2026. Targets, not results.

  5. Apr 2026

    Google: 75% of all new code is AI-generated and approved by engineers.

  6. May 2026

    • Anthropic: more than 80% of merged code authored by Claude, up from low single digits before Claude Code (Feb 2025).
    • Salesforce: no net new engineers in FY26, ~20% more salespeople, about $300M on Anthropic tokens in 2026.

Productivity is unclear

  • METR's Feb 2026 follow-up estimates an 18% speedup, which METR calls "very weak evidence".
  • Anthropic: engineers merged 8x more code per day in Q2 2026 than in 2024, "almost certainly an overstatement" of real gains.
  • Human code review is now the bottleneck at Anthropic.

Developers verify, not trust

  • 66% spend more time fixing "almost right" AI code (SO 2025).
  • Only 6.6% trust AI for important decisions (SO 2026, 30,903 respondents).
  • Indian developers trust AI most: 56% highly or somewhat.

Entry-level jobs take the hit

  • US developers aged 22–25: down nearly 20% from the late-2022 peak; the exposed-job gap reached 19% by Jun 2026 (Stanford).
  • Indeed software index 74.4 (Feb 2020 = 100); 71% of the rebound is senior roles.
  • CS grad underemployment is low (19.1%): those hired mostly land degree-level jobs.

Indian IT is shrinking at the bottom

  • TCS headcount fell by 23,460 in FY26 to 5,84,519.
  • Top-5 IT firms: net −6,981 in FY26, against +12,718 in FY25.
  • TCS guides 25,000 freshers for FY27, down from the usual 40,000–42,000; Infosys about 20,000.

Key insight

Coding is becoming a cheap commodity. Open models like DeepSeek V4 Pro reach 96.4% on SWE-bench Verified at about $0.10 a task. The scarce human value is judgment, review, system design, domain knowledge and owning outcomes.
